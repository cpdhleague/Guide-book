{% include card-grid.html cards="Sailors' Bane" %}

## Summary

[[Sailors' Bane]] is a voltron-control deck that has spent time in the top 10 ranked pauper commanders on our [meta page](/meta/). It is fast, well protected, and resilient. The commander has been banned in PDC (Pauper Duel Commander) due to being problematic in French PDC circuit.

Because the commander needs so few cards to get set up, I can dedicate a huge proportion of the deck to countermagic and removal. My clock lives in the command zone and my 99 buy me time to take out my opponents.

## Why Bane is So Strong

Sailors' Bane costs {7}{U}{U} for a 7/7 with ward {4}, and it costs {1} less for each card you own in exile and in your graveyard that is an instant, a sorcery, or a card with an Adventure. A few benefits I get from the command zone:

**The cost reduction counts exile as well as the graveyard.** Graveyard hate does not shut me off the way it shuts off a deck like [[Hollow Marauder]]. It'll take away any flashback spells I may have had waiting for me, but it won't strand my commander in the command zone. 

**Ward {4} is a lot of protection for a commander this cheap.** I do not need to spend card slots on protection spells, and I do not need to play around removal the way a fragile voltron deck does. Even if they pay their way through the ward, the commander will often cost {U}{U}, making it easy to recast right away.

**7 power is no joke.** I have more than a few ways to give this number a slight bump to turn my commander damage into a two-turn executioner. In addition, blue is the king of making creatures unblockable, so the first players I target may not be able to stop me from taking them out of the game. 

## Deck Composition

### 1. Mill

A bunch of the cards in my deck are there for one reason: to get Bane on the battlefield on turn three - or even turn two when I get lucky! A bunch of these are cards that say "mill four" or "mill five" but other cards, like [[Tolarian Winds]], [[Foresight]], or [[Otherworldly Gaze]] are serving the same function despite not having "mill" in their oracle text.

[[Foresight]] is one of my favorite cards to cast on turn two. It replaces itself, and it exiles three cards from my library - three mill cards I would love to see in my opening hand, but hate to see later in the game.

{% include card-grid.html cards="Foresight; Tolarian Winds; Tome Scour; Stream of Thought; Careful Study; Shriekhorn" %}

### 2. Evasion & Buff

Once Bane is on the board, I need small buffs on it to make it a two-turn kill and to get it through any blockers my opponents have started putting in my way.

The buffs matter quite a bit to the efficiency of the deck. Without them, I can take down the table in 9 turns at my fastest. By ensuring I can double tap my opponents, it'll take me just 6 turns, allowing me to win on turn 9 if all goes to plan (it never does).

Sometimes I don't get the buff on time, and that can be okay. I can hit an opponent for 7 now knowing I have ways to give Bane +2 power and come back for a killing blow in a turn or two. 

{% include card-grid.html cards="Spectral Flight; Cartouche of Knowledge; One With the Wind; Distortion Strike; Maximize Altitude; Security Bypass" %}

### 3. Counterspells & Removal

I counter spells and I remove permanents that pose me problems or death. I think this part is pretty simple. The complexities arise from the pod composition and knowing both your meta and the gameplans of opposing decks so you know who and what to prioritize. 

Casting my commander and giving it the small buffs to make it lethal fast are easy to unfold. A lot of the skill ceiling of this deck will be reached through understanding pod dynamics, meta decks, being able to identify the gameplan of a deck you haven't seen before, on the spot - the sort of mental traits that come with playing PDH for some time. I believe the best way to master this deck is to play PDH and learn more about the meta. This applies to all decks, of course, but more so for controlling decks that need to pick apart their opponents to win. 

{% include card-grid.html cards="Counterspell; Logic Knot; Memory Lapse; Muddle the Mixture; Echoing Truth; Snap" %}

## Opening Hands

My number one priority is to get the commander out as soon as I can. Keep a hand with a clear path to casting Sailors' Bane on turn three, or, if you draw a hand with [[Gitaxian Probe]] or [[Snapback]], the enviable turn two cast.

## Notes & Strategies

- I like Delve. I use the graveyard without fear of stranding the commander.
- I kill my opponents one at a time. It may feel heartless, but it stops them developing resources to use against me later. I take out the players first who will cause me the most headaches (or death) in the late game. Combo players are always a priority. 
- I run several "cast from graveyard" effects that allow me to turn the mill from my first turns into card access for later
- Creatures with adventures also reduce my commander cost! Don't overlook them.
