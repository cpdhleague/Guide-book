This might be the easiest deck tech I ever write. [[Dionus, Elvish Archdruid]] is so simple to pilot - and fun to boot! On top of that, I believe this Elf Tribal deck is the strongest mono-green commander in cPDH.

{% include card-grid.html cards="Dionus, Elvish Archdruid" %}

## Summary

Dionus's untap ability lets me ramp hard in the first turns, giving me the mana to unload my hand onto the board. Once my hand is empty, the commander ability lets me swing with abandon - my untapped board provides mana for instants in addition to meaningful blockers. All the while, the untapping shenanigans give my board quick boosts in power to heighten my threat level and boosts in toughness to increase my survivability.

Dionus won its first tournament in September. You can see the full [decklist here](https://moxfield.com/decks/yN3MTRw0DEqUtxLO6oktHA) and listen in to my conversation with Fies, who piloted the deck to victory [here](https://cpdh.guide/tournament%20reports/tryhard-brawl-interview/).

My list varies a little from Fies' list, but the truth is that there is a lot of flexibility in what you bring to the list. As long as you've got 50+ elves that are cheap or have strong effects, you're ready to rumble in my experience. Even Fies, who championed this deck with great success, made his deck with what he had at home, without the utmost optimization, and that was more than enough for him!

## Gameplan

### 1. Mulligan for Mana

I find it important to see a mana dork in my opening hand so I can cast my commander on turn three. Since there is so much redundancy in the deck, mana is the one thing I need to worry about to get up and running.

### 2. Cast Dionus by Turn 3

I dedicate turns 1 and 2 to putting all of my mana sources onto the battlefield. All of my mana dorks do wonders for me after Dionus has resolved, but I need them in play by then.

{% include card-grid.html cards="Citanul Stalwart; Birchlore Rangers; Elvish Mystic; Arbor Elf; Priest of Titania" %}

Then, on turn 3, I cast Dionus. Sometimes, if I'm lucky, I'll still have some mana left over to put even more elves in play or hold up one of my few instants.

### 3. Elf Ball

I don't know that this needs much explanation. Play as many elves as you can each turn. Tap as many of them as you can to maximize your Dionus triggers. Right after you've played Dionus, focus on emptying your hand. If you're lucky enough to hit one of my big draw spells that can net you up to five creatures (gotta be lucky to get all of them), you can just focus on continuing to dump and buff your guys out of [[Breath Weapon]] range.

{% include card-grid.html cards="Lead the Stampede; Beast Hunt; You Meet in a Tavern; Winding Way; Tapping at the Window" %}

Once you have most of your hand on the board, you can switch your focus from mana generation to maximum damage output, giving your elves pseudo-vigilance as they untap each attack.

### 4. Late Game Cards

As the game nears its end, I tend to have more mana than cards to play. For this reason, I've included a handful of mana sinks to give my excess mana somewhere to go later on.

{% include card-grid.html cards="Wildheart Invoker; Sprout Swarm; Nyxborn Hydra" %}

In addition, once all of my guys have several counters on them, there are several cards that give all my creatures with +1/+1 counters trample. I run those because they help me close out the game through any blockers my opponents may have established.

{% include card-grid.html cards="Pridemalkin; Duskshell Crawler; Gnarlid Colony; Ferocious Werefox" %}

At any time, if an opponent is close to comboing off or winning by other means, I want to make sure I keep open mana for my slim interaction package. Several instants allow me to deal damage to an opponent's creatures equal to the power of my creatures. I try to avoid fight spells as they put my own creatures at risk of being removed once weakened, except for the ones that cost just 1 mana and can be clutch in narrow mana situations.

{% include card-grid.html cards="Titanic Brawl; Ram Through; Cosmic Hunger; Mutant's Prey; Rocky Rebuke" %}

[[Scattershot Archer]] can kill Malcolm on my turn and [[Savaen Elves]] destroys land enchantments from Simic combo decks. I run them for those matchups.
